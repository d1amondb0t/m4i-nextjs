Final Evaluation: Citizen Voice in Municipal Budgets Programme, Riverton Municipality (2019-2024)

Executive summary

The Citizen Voice in Municipal Budgets Programme ran from January 2019 to June 2024 in Riverton, a municipality of about 210,000 residents. It was implemented by the Civic Futures Trust with funding from the Northern Governance Fund. The programme aimed to give residents a direct say over part of the municipal capital budget and to make participation in local decision-making more inclusive, especially for women and residents of the informal settlements on the eastern edge of the city.

The evaluation finds that the programme met or exceeded most of its targets. Participatory budgeting is now written into municipal law, a growing share of the capital budget is allocated by residents, and turnout in the 2023 municipal election rose compared with 2018. Gains in the representation of women in elected office were smaller than planned.

Programme design and results framework

The programme's theory of change was that regular, accessible neighbourhood assemblies would increase participation, that a successful pilot would persuade the municipal council to make participatory budgeting permanent, and that visible results would raise trust in the council and electoral participation over time. The results framework set out the following indicators, baselines and targets.

Short-term outcomes (outputs and immediate results)
Indicator 1.1: Number of neighbourhood budget assemblies held. Baseline: 0. Target: 30. Result: 32 assemblies were held across all 14 wards between 2019 and 2023.
Indicator 1.2: Number of residents taking part in assemblies and online voting. Baseline: 0. Target: 3,000. Result: 4,100 residents took part in assemblies and a further 6,800 voted online on project shortlists.
Indicator 1.3: Share of assembly participants who are women. Baseline: not applicable. Target: 40%. Result: 44% of assembly participants were women.
Indicator 1.4: Number of civil society organisations registered as assembly partners. Target: 20. Result: 27 organisations, including 9 women's groups and 5 residents' associations from the informal settlements.

Medium-term outcomes
Indicator 2.1: Participatory budgeting made permanent in municipal regulation. Baseline: no legal basis. Target: ordinance adopted by 2022. Result: the municipal council adopted the Participatory Budgeting Ordinance in September 2022 by 31 votes to 6. The ordinance requires at least 15% of the annual capital budget to be allocated through participatory budgeting.
Indicator 2.2: Share of the municipal capital budget allocated by residents. Baseline: 0%. Target: 15%. Result: 18% in the 2024 budget, equal to 6.2 million dollars.
Indicator 2.3: Share of resident-selected projects completed within 18 months. Target: 70%. Result: 64%. Delays in procurement held back road and drainage projects.
Indicator 2.4: Share of women on the municipal Budget Oversight Committee. Baseline: 17%. Target: 40%. Result: 36%.

Long-term outcomes
Indicator 3.1: Turnout in municipal elections. Baseline: 41% in 2018. Target: 47% in 2023. Result: 49% in the 2023 municipal election, with the largest increase in the eastern wards (from 29% to 40%).
Indicator 3.2: Trust in the municipal council, measured on a 1-5 scale in the annual residents' survey. Baseline: 2.9 in 2019. Target: 3.3. Result: 3.4 in 2024.
Indicator 3.3: Share of council seats held by women. Baseline: 19% after the 2018 election. Target: 30%. Result: 24% after the 2023 election.

How consultation results were used

Each assembly ranked project proposals, and the top-ranked proposals were costed by the municipal planning department before a city-wide online vote. In 2024, 41 resident-selected projects were included in the capital budget, mostly street lighting, drainage, school repairs and two health posts. The planning department now publishes a yearly report showing which proposals were funded and why others were not.

Civic space

Local civil society organisations reported no restrictions on their activities during the programme. Two neighbourhood newspapers and a community radio station covered the assemblies. In 2021, the council also opened its committee meetings to the public and began streaming them online.

Lessons learned

Childcare and evening meeting times were the main reasons for higher participation by women. Online voting extended reach but favoured younger, wealthier residents, so the assemblies remain necessary. The programme recommends that the council fund the participatory budgeting secretariat from its own budget after the donor funding ends in 2024; the council has included the secretariat's staff costs in its draft 2025 budget.
