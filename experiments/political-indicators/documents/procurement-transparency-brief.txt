Project Brief: Open Contracting for Public Works in the Republic of Lorvania (Phase 1, 2021-2023)

Background

Public works account for about a third of government spending in Lorvania. Before the project, tenders were advertised in print only, contract awards were not published, and the Supreme Audit Office had repeatedly reported weak record keeping in the Ministry of Infrastructure's procurement unit. The project was designed with the Ministry of Finance and the Public Procurement Agency to make public works procurement more transparent and to strengthen oversight.

What the project did

The project supported the Public Procurement Agency in launching an electronic procurement portal in March 2022. From July 2022 all tenders by the Ministry of Infrastructure above 50,000 dollars have been advertised on the portal, and award notices are published there with the winning bid and the names of the companies that bid.

A training programme for public officials ran throughout 2022. In total, 140 procurement officers from the Ministry of Infrastructure and four regional public works departments completed a five-day course on procurement planning, bid evaluation and contract management. A further 35 internal auditors were trained on auditing electronic procurement records.

The project drafted amendments to the Public Procurement Law that make publication of contracts mandatory and create an independent complaints review panel. The cabinet approved the draft in November 2022, and parliament passed the amended law in May 2023. The complaints review panel has been appointed but had not yet heard any cases by the end of the project.

At the request of the Ministry of Finance, the Supreme Audit Office carried out a performance audit of public works procurement in 2022. The audit made 12 recommendations to the Ministry of Infrastructure. By the end of 2023, the ministry reported that it had fully implemented 7 of them, including keeping complete tender files and separating evaluation and approval duties.

Oversight and civil society

The project funded a coalition of three civil society organisations to monitor a sample of 60 road contracts using the data published on the portal. Their first monitoring report, released in October 2023, identified price overruns on 9 contracts and was discussed at a hearing of the parliamentary public accounts committee.

Next steps

Phase 2, planned for 2024-2026, will extend the portal to the health and education ministries and will begin measuring whether open contracting changes prices and competition. Phase 1 did not measure changes in corruption or in public trust; a baseline survey of businesses' perceptions of procurement integrity is planned for early 2024.
